# DemonX Forge 0.17.0

Mesh to Solid, our own converter.

- **Mesh to Solid rebuilds a mesh as real sketches and extrudes.** It finds the direction in which every face of the part is either flat across it or straight along it, cuts the mesh into slabs at the flat levels, turns each slab's outline into sketch lines, arcs and circles (holes and islands told apart), and extrudes them. What you get in the timeline is a normal sketch and extrude for each slab that you can double-click and change, not a skin of triangles. A lipped strip with three holes and a rounded corner comes back with the same volume as the part it was exported from.
- **What it rebuilds:** plates, steps, lips, ribs, round holes, slots and rounded corners, in the part's X, Y or Z direction. **What it refuses** (with the reason, changing nothing): slanted or tapered faces across the thickness, countersinks, spheres, sculpted shapes, and parts that need more than one direction at once (a bracket with a hole through the base and a profile along its length).
- **A check:** the rebuilt solid's volume must match the mesh's to within 1%; if it does not, everything is undone and the engine's own prism converter is tried instead. If both refuse, you get both reasons.
- **One Ctrl+Z** takes the whole conversion back (and Ctrl+Y brings it again), although it is several steps underneath.

**Tested:** unit tests for the outline-to-arcs step; the real engine for a lipped strip (volume, faces, undo and redo), a chamfered part along X (a prism), a part that cannot be rebuilt (refused, nothing changed); the browser check `e2e/meshimport.mjs`. **Not tested:** meshes from other programs (their tessellation can differ), meshes of hundreds of thousands of triangles, Windows.
